# indie-saas-financial-baseline

Money discipline for solo SaaS operators. The boring infrastructure work that keeps an indie business out of trouble with the IRS, with banks, and with itself.

Mirrors the structure of `security-legal-baseline`: standing rules you set up once, plus operational rhythms you run forever.

## Why this exists

Most "indie founder finance" content is either:

- Generic "track your expenses" advice with no specifics, or
- VC-startup advice that doesn't apply to bootstrappers, or
- Tax-prep services trying to sell you their software

This is the operational reference: what to set up, what to track, what to ignore, what to revisit monthly.

## How to use

Top to bottom on day 1, or as soon as possible if you've already started. Each file is a one-time setup followed by an ongoing rhythm.

## Files

- `README.md` (this file)
- `01-separate-accounts.md` - business bank, business card, why before anything else
- `02-legal-entity-decision.md` - sole prop, LLC, S-corp, when to upgrade
- `03-sales-tax-nexus.md` - US states, EU VAT, what triggers nexus for SaaS
- `04-cogs-tracking.md` - per-user COGS for AI-cost-heavy businesses
- `05-runway-math.md` - cash on hand, burn, what counts as runway
- `06-byok-pricing-logic.md` - when BYOK preserves margin, when bundled is right
- `07-tax-fundamentals.md` - quarterly estimated tax, deductible categories
- `08-monthly-review.md` - the 30-minute monthly money review procedure
- `09-when-to-incorporate.md` - revenue, liability, hiring triggers
- `10-what-to-avoid.md` - commingling, late filings, ignoring sales tax

## Disclaimer

This is operational guidance, not legal or tax advice. Talk to a CPA, talk to a lawyer, talk to a tax professional for your specific situation. Especially if your business crosses jurisdictions, has employees, or is taking on significant revenue.
